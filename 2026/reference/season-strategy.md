# 2026 In-Season Strategy

> **Purpose:** Durable decision policy for managing the HAL 9000s after the draft.  
> **Authority:** `league-rules.md` governs league mechanics. Current ESPN state and verified current news govern changing facts.

## Objective

Maximize weekly points and season-long championship probability through active roster management. The drafted roster is starting inventory, not a commitment.

## Decision Priorities

1. Keep every starting slot filled with an active, eligible player.
2. Protect elite and scarce weekly advantages.
3. Acquire players whose role, volume, or injury-created opportunity can materially grow.
4. Improve the weakest usable roster spot rather than collecting redundant names.
5. Plan for imminent byes and game-time uncertainty without sacrificing clearly superior long-term value.
6. Stream D/ST and kicker when a meaningful weekly upgrade is available.
7. Preserve flexibility for late injury news and unexpected breakouts.

## Waivers and Free Agency

This league has no season acquisition limit and resets waiver priority weekly to inverse standings. There is no durable benefit to saving priority.

- Be aggressive when a player projects as a real starter, strong FLEX, or high-upside injury replacement.
- Rank claims as complete **add/drop pairs** so every contingency is executable.
- Distinguish a submitted claim from a successful claim.
- Do not drop a valuable player solely to cover a one-week marginal projection.
- Prefer uncertain players with plausible expanding roles over low-ceiling bench insulation.
- Before recommending a drop, test whether the player would become an obvious claim if another manager released him.
- Consider upcoming byes and roster locks before selecting the drop candidate.
- Use free agency after waivers to repair failed claims or make low-cost streaming moves.

## Lineup Decisions

Evaluate players using role and opportunity first, then matchup.

- Volume, routes, targets, carries, red-zone work, snap share, and quarterback context matter more than one-week box scores.
- Start elite players unless health or role has materially changed.
- Use FLEX timing: place the latest-starting eligible player in FLEX when practical.
- Move Thursday players out of FLEX into their positional slots when eligible.
- Treat questionable players by game time, replacement quality, and the availability of later alternatives.
- Recheck the lineup after final injury reports and before the Sunday early window.
- Avoid chasing projected points when stronger evidence supports a different choice.

## Roster Construction

- One starting QB and TE generally make replacement value accessible, but elite production at either position should be protected.
- Favor RB/WR bench depth with paths to starting volume.
- A second QB or TE must solve a specific bye, injury, or material value opportunity.
- Avoid carrying multiple D/STs or kickers unless a near-term schedule or transaction constraint clearly justifies it.
- Use the IR slot whenever an eligible retained player would otherwise consume a bench slot.
- Bye-week collisions matter most among actual starters; they are a planning constraint, not a reason to weaken the roster weeks in advance.

## Trades

Judge trades by the strongest starting lineup after the deal, replacement cost, depth risk, and playoff utility.

- Prefer consolidating replaceable depth into a meaningful starter upgrade.
- Do not value a trade by raw player count.
- Account for the December 2 deadline and one-day review period.
- Record offers separately from accepted and processed trades.

## Evidence and Uncertainty

Current information can change quickly.

- Verify injuries, suspensions, depth-chart changes, and schedule facts using current sources.
- State uncertainty explicitly when reports conflict or roles are unsettled.
- Separate observed facts from inference.
- Do not let old repository notes override newer ESPN or NFL evidence.
- Never state that a transaction occurred until the user or a fresh ESPN snapshot confirms it.

## Weekly Review Cadence

### Opening review

- Confirm current roster, matchup, record, standings, waiver position, injuries, and byes.
- Identify expendable roster spots and the week's principal risks.
- Build waiver claims and fallback add/drop pairs.

### Post-waiver review

- Confirm which claims succeeded.
- Update the roster and transaction log.
- Repair gaps through free agency.

### Lineup review

- Reassess before Thursday kickoff.
- Reassess after final injury reports and before Sunday kickoff.
- Flag late-window or prime-time contingency options for questionable players.

### Closeout

- Record the final lineup, result, transactions, important decisions, and lessons.
- Carry only durable unresolved items into the next week.
